2015 — The Spark
Two batchmates stuck in Bangalore traffic, asking the same question over and over.

Utkarsh Kawatra (ex-Helion Ventures) and Vinayak Aggarwal (ex-Goldman Sachs) had known each other from IIT Delhi. After college, life took them their separate ways. Then Bangalore happened.

They'd meet up after work, sit in traffic, grab dinner — and the same frustration kept coming up. Why can't people work from wherever they actually want to? That question is where the idea of myHQ came from. Not a whiteboard session. Not a pitch deck. Just two people stuck in traffic, talking honestly about something that bothered them.

Early 2016 — The Move
They had a friend with a café. The café had a problem. They saw an opportunity.

Once they decided this was worth pursuing, they needed a place to start. A friend of theirs ran a café in Delhi NCR, so they made the move to the capital.

The café had a pretty common problem — it was dead during the day. Chairs up, tables empty, the place just sitting there burning rent between breakfast and the evening rush. Most people would see that and think nothing of it. Utkarsh and Vinayak looked at those empty chairs and saw a workspace.

That's honestly where myHQ starts. Not some grand founding moment — just two guys looking at an empty café and thinking, what if people could work here?

The Origin
April 2016 — Founded
Free WiFi. Free coffee. Discounted menus. A place to make magic. No strings.

The model wasn't complicated. Come in, get free WiFi, order at a discounted rate, work in a space that actually felt good to be in. No deposit. No monthly commitment. No awkward "are you going to order something?" energy. Just show up and work.

The café that used to sit empty before noon started filling up. Freelancers, early founders, remote workers — people who just needed somewhere that felt right. It worked. And that taught them the most important thing they'd carry forward: idle space is wasted potential.

2016–17 — First Legs
No marketing budget. Just word of mouth. And it was enough.

They didn't run ads. There was no launch campaign. People just started telling other people.

One café became two. Then five. Eventually they were running the same model across 20+ cafés and pubs in Delhi NCR — places like Social, Philips MTV, CCD. Different venues, same idea: come work here during the day, we'll handle the rest. The concept had legs. Now it was time for wings.

2017–18 — Growing Up
Cafés were the start. Coworking spaces were the next step. The Day Pass is born.

As demand kept coming, it became clear that cafés could only take them so far. Coworking spaces offered more structure, more inventory, more scale.

They moved fast. In a short time, 100+ coworking spaces were listed on the platform. And with that shift, a new product emerged — the Day Pass. Book a day, show up, work, leave. Pay for what you use. Nothing more.

Clients started asking for more. So they started building more.

As more businesses started using the platform, the requests got more varied. People wanted their company registered in another state. They wanted a proper office address. They needed more than a day pass could give them.

2020–21 — The Test
COVID shut everything down. Multiple buyout offers came in. They said no to every single one.

When the pandemic hit, it didn't ease myHQ into a tough patch — it just turned the lights off. Cafés shut. Coworking spaces closed. Revenue dropped hard.

Several offers came in from people who were happy to buy the company out of its trouble. The founders turned all of them down.

Then ANAROCK came along. Not just as a rescue — but as the right fit. Deep expertise in Indian real estate. The biggest CRE advisory platform in the country. They understood the space in a way that actually mattered.

myHQ became myHQ by ANAROCK. The founders stayed. The mission stayed. What changed was everything else.
